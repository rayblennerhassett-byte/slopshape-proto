# The night the bookmobile broke down

By Tom Osei

When I first joined the county library, the bookmobile was the only branch most people had ever met. I remember my first week: the lift jammed outside the senior center, and Mrs. Alvarez, who runs the quilting circle, asked me whether the new route would still come on Thursdays. I told her I hoped so, and then I spent three weeks finding out that nobody had mapped the roads the driver actually drove.

Our driver, Rosa Jiménez, said the paper schedule dated from 2011. I asked the county IT office what they had, and they said a spreadsheet. Maybe that was fair — the roads change after rain, and the spreadsheet didn't. I suspect the gap between the schedule and the mud is where most rural services quietly fail.

So we stopped printing the old route and started calling the stops. To be fair, the first month was messy: two cancellations, one flat tire, and a lot of apologizing. The second month, attendance was up from roughly nine visitors a week to thirty, and the quilting circle had a waiting list.

Probably the lesson is boring: ask the people who ride, not the people who file. I often think libraries measure the wrong things because the right ones are hard to count. I don't know the answer, but I'd rather hear it from Rosa than from a dashboard.
