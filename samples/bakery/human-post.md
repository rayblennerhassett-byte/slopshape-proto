# The bread line said more than the dashboard did

By Ana Duarte

Last winter I nearly closed the bakery. December had been warm, the flour price jumped, and I kept telling myself the line outside was just holiday noise. My first month here, I'd done everything by the spreadsheet — order when stock hit zero, bake when the oven was free. The spreadsheet didn't know that Tuesday regulars buy the rye bread their grandmother made, or that a rainy Saturday empties the shop by two.

In November, our supplier M. Okafor told me he'd raised prices twice in one quarter. I asked him why. He said the wheat futures market doesn't care about small bakeries. I suspect he was right, and I also suspect most shop owners learn this the expensive way.

So we changed the baking schedule, not the menu. Rye twice a week instead of daily, plus a sourdough on Fridays. To be fair, the first Friday was slow — maybe the regulars were away. The second Friday, the rye sold out by noon, up from roughly half a loaf the previous Tuesday.

Maybe that's the whole lesson: the line outside tells you what to bake, and the spreadsheet only tells you what sold last month. I often wonder how many other shops are reading the wrong numbers. I don't know — but I'd rather ask the people in the queue.
